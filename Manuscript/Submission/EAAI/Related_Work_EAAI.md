## A. Benchmarking and Design-Factor Attribution in RUL Prediction

The NASA CMAPSS benchmark [1], released in 2008, established the canonical testbed for data-driven turbofan prognostics and remains the primary evaluation platform for RUL prediction research. Heimes [2] demonstrated that recurrent architectures could track degradation trends from multivariate sensor sequences, establishing an early FD001 RMSE ceiling above 25 cycles. Zheng et al. [4] introduced the stacked two-layer LSTM that became the dominant sequential backbone; Li et al. [5] combined LSTM temporal modelling with convolutional feature extraction to reduce FD001 RMSE into the 13–15 cycle range. Subsequent architectural advances — including bidirectional recurrent models, Transformer encoders, graph neural networks, and hybrid attention systems — have continued this trajectory, with recent studies reporting RMSE values in the low-to-mid teens under heterogeneous evaluation protocols [12, 13, 25].

Despite this apparent progress, a systematic attribution problem persists. The prevailing evaluation pattern bundles preprocessing, backbone architecture, and training objective into a single proposed system and reports aggregate performance without isolating contributions from individual stages. Deng et al. [25], for instance, combined CNN feature extraction, LSTM temporal modelling, and multi-head attention into a unified system reporting RMSE = 13.91 on FD003; Asif et al. [38] showed that refined RUL labelling and sensor preprocessing alone substantially reduce the performance gap on FD001 without any architectural modification. In both cases, the source of the reported gain cannot be attributed to any single pipeline component. Ramasso and Saxena [3], reviewing more than seventy CMAPSS publications, found that absent standardised preprocessing baselines and persistent dataset misunderstandings, multi-fault-mode and operating-condition challenges remained unresolved despite years of collective effort. Li et al. [15] reached the same conclusion in a recent survey, noting that preprocessing choices are rarely disclosed in sufficient detail for independent reproduction. Existing CMAPSS studies demonstrate strong predictive performance, but their bundled experimental designs make it difficult to determine whether reported gains originate from target construction, normalisation, architecture, or loss design.

## B. RUL Label Construction and Sensor Normalisation

**RUL label construction.** Piecewise-linear RUL labelling with a degradation-onset ceiling is the most widely adopted formulation in the CMAPSS literature [4, 5]. The specific ceiling threshold used by individual studies ranges from 100 to 150 cycles without systematic justification across sub-datasets. Ensarioğlu et al. [17] showed that change-point detection-based labelling improves FD001 RMSE, but did not evaluate the ceiling threshold as an independent variable. Imbert et al. [16] found that preprocessing choices — including RUL label specification — drove a substantial share of observed performance gains in temporal convolutional network experiments. Asif et al. [38] reached a compatible conclusion for LSTM-based models: labelling and sensor preprocessing alone substantially closed the FD001 performance gap without architectural modification. These findings indicate that the clipping ceiling functions as a high-leverage prerequisite design decision, yet, to the best of our knowledge, no prior study has conducted a controlled multi-threshold ablation across all four CMAPSS sub-datasets while holding the backbone architecture constant.

**Sensor normalisation.** Fleet-level min-max scaling applied over all training engines is the default normalisation in dominant CMAPSS implementations [4, 5, 8], but alternatives — per-unit z-score, per-unit min-max with an initial-window baseline, and instance-level distribution-shift correction — have been proposed. The operating-condition heterogeneity of FD002 and FD004, where sensors shift by tens to hundreds of units across six discrete operating regimes, complicates the direct application of fleet-level statistics and has motivated condition-aware preprocessing frameworks [9, 21]. Reversible Instance Normalisation (RevIN) [20], originally developed for non-stationary time-series forecasting, performs per-window mean and variance normalisation followed by a learnable affine rescaling of model outputs, and has shown strong gains in distribution-shift settings. Recent analyses question whether its benefits extend to settings where the primary modelling challenge is degradation-trend extraction rather than temporal distribution shift [22, 23]; instance-normalisation flows [24] present a related construction for non-stationary time series that similarly lacks evaluation in the prognostic domain. Despite this variety, to the best of our knowledge, no published study compares fleet-level, per-unit, and instance-level normalisation under a controlled, backbone-matched protocol applied simultaneously to all four CMAPSS sub-datasets.

## C. Multi-Fault Prognostics and Routing Architectures

FD003 and FD004 contain engines degrading under two distinct failure modes — high-pressure compressor (HPC) and fan degradation — documented in early CMAPSS work [6] and confirmed through cluster quality analyses in subsequent studies [31, 32]. Despite this structure, the majority of published CMAPSS models apply a single shared architecture to all engines without architectural distinction between single-fault and multi-fault sub-datasets.

Several routing and mixture-of-experts strategies have been explored to exploit fault-mode structure in prognostics. Lodygowski and Szrama [27] combined autoencoders with Gaussian Mixture Models (GMM) for unsupervised failure-mode identification, treating cluster assignment as a preprocessing step prior to model training. Liu et al. [28] applied a mixture-of-encoders architecture conditioned on operating regime, demonstrating improved performance on multi-condition datasets. Hard-routing architectures — in which each engine is assigned exclusively to one expert branch based on predicted fault mode — have been applied in the multi-condition prognostics literature [29], but their sensitivity to routing error at test time has not been isolated from other design choices. Yang et al. [40] suggest, in the context of multisource domain adaptation for fault diagnosis, that metric-learning-based gating may degrade when the distributional consistency of gating features between training and deployment is not maintained — a reliability concern directly applicable to trajectory-derived routing features.

The observation horizon available at routing time is a critical constraint on these approaches. Wang et al. [45] suggest that accurate fault-mode recognition benefits substantially from partial supervisory labels, with purely unsupervised mode identification substantially less reliable in complex degradation settings. Dong et al. [44] suggest, based on causal-inference experiments on N-CMAPSS, that early anomaly detection is difficult in realistic turbofan data due to nonlinear thermodynamic couplings and complex degradation dynamics. These limitations motivate approaches that defer routing until sufficient degradation history has accumulated: Fu et al. [42] applied trajectory clustering over complete run-to-failure histories to identify failure modes, and Wang et al. [43] proposed a joint fault-recognition prognostic framework that processes the full temporal sequence before routing. Full-trajectory strategies improve routing reliability by leveraging richer evidence, but require extended observation windows at test time. The reliability boundary among hard partitioning, soft posterior-weighted routing, and end-to-end early-cycle gating trained jointly with the prognostic objective has not been systematically evaluated under matched prognostic conditions across both multi-fault CMAPSS sub-datasets.

## D. Loss Engineering and Asymmetric Prognostic Risk

The NASA prognostic score penalises late predictions exponentially more than early ones, reflecting the asymmetric cost structure of aviation maintenance decisions. This motivates training objectives that embed a similar asymmetry. Rengasamy et al. [33, 34] introduced dynamically weighted and asymmetric loss variants that improved NASA score on individual CMAPSS sub-datasets; Liu et al. [35] proposed a multi-head network with asymmetric constraints, reporting FD001 gains; Abdullah [19] combined asymmetric loss with attention-based interpretability, again demonstrating single-dataset improvement. Quantile regression approaches — including Pinball loss [36], theoretically grounded calibrated interval estimation [37], and quantile mixture-of-experts formulations [39] — provide probabilistic prediction intervals suited to risk-averse decision-making, at the cost of point-estimate RMSE and without direct optimisation for the NASA score.

A consistent limitation across these studies is the assumption of a fixed RUL-labelling scheme, typically piecewise-linear with a threshold near 125 cycles, without systematic variation of that threshold. Chung et al. [37] showed that minimising Pinball loss alone does not guarantee calibrated quantile estimates. In the present setting, the extreme range of uncapped FD003 targets may further complicate stable quantile estimation; this is treated as an interpretation rather than a tested mechanism. The interaction between the RUL clipping threshold and loss-function performance has not been evaluated under a cross-dataset design with multiple-comparison correction, leaving open whether reported single-dataset gains from custom loss functions remain statistically detectable after the clipping condition is properly controlled.

## E. Research Gaps and Study Positioning

Four specific attribution gaps emerge from this review:

1. **RUL label clipping:** No prior study has varied the ceiling threshold as an isolated factor across all four CMAPSS sub-datasets, preventing quantification of its magnitude relative to architectural or loss-function effects.
2. **Sensor normalisation:** Fleet-level, per-unit, and instance-level strategies have not been compared under a controlled, backbone-matched protocol across all four sub-datasets simultaneously.
3. **Fault-mode routing:** The reliability boundary among hard partitioning, soft posterior-weighted routing, and end-to-end early-cycle gating has not been directly evaluated under matched prognostic conditions on both multi-fault CMAPSS sub-datasets.
4. **Loss–clipping interaction:** Whether custom loss functions provide statistically detectable improvements over MSE after the RUL clipping condition is controlled and multiple comparisons are corrected remains unestablished.

Accordingly, this study evaluates four pipeline factors through hypothesis-specific experiments (H1–H4), with each focal factor varied within its respective controlled setting. A supplementary protocol-unified N1–N3 analysis assesses the robustness of the principal normalization comparison, and the resulting evidence is synthesised into an attribution-oriented staged checklist for PHM practitioners.
